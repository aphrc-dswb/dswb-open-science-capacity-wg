## Notes

### Definition of Sovereignty

- The state as a whole (Old French *Souverain*)
- The absolute and perpetual power of the state, indivisible, above the laws it makes. (Bodin)
- The Peace of Westphalia ties supreme authority to territory and establishes non-interference between states, the foundation of the modern state system.
- Rousseau and the age of revolutions relocate sovereignty from crown to people.
- The UN Charter (1945) makes states sovereign equals; decolonization makes it Africa's claim to self-rule.
- Sovereignty is the right to decide; autonomy is the ability to do so.
- What determines the ability in any era is who controls the right of production.

### Capability Under Interdependence

- Internal supremacy and external independence, but exercised amid interdependence:
  - Pooled where useful (EU, AU, AfCFTA)
  - Read as responsibility (R2P, 2005)
  - Extended to new domains: economic, digital, data

### Revolution Ages

- Agrarian
- Industrial
- Informational
- AI & Robotics

> If you own it, you have sovereignty and autonomy. If you rent it, you have just autonomy.

### Data Sovereignty: Three Questions That Decide Everything

- **Who controls the data?**
  - Are the people who own this data benefiting and getting value from it, monetary and otherwise?
- **Under whose law is it processed?**
- **Who captures the value?**

### Data Governance and Trustworthy AI

There are six layers to do this:

- **Data:** Where data resides and under whose law it is processed
- **Compute:** Owned or guaranteed training and inference hardware on African territory
- **Models:** Ownership or unrestricted modification rights over weights used in critical functions
- **Talent:** A domestic workforce able to build, audit and govern systems
- **Energy:** The ability to power the stack without importing vulnerability
- **Governance:** The capacity to set and enforce rules on systems in the jurisdiction

### Properties of Trustworthy AI

These are also data-governance properties:

- **Accuracy & reliability:** Data quality standards, representative sampling, documented provenance
- **Privacy & consent:** Lawful basis, purpose limitation, minimization, governed at collection, not patched at deployment
- **Fairness & non-discrimination:** Bias audits of training data; representation of African languages and populations
- **Accountability & auditability:** Data lineage, access logs, model documentation; you cannot audit what you cannot trace
- **Safety & security:** Access controls, breach response, and defined sensitive classes under defined jurisdiction

### Four Documented Cases

Legal Architecture Exists, but the Domestication Lags

**Malabo Convention (2014; in force 2023)**

- The AU Convention on Cyber Security and Personal Data Protection
- The continent's binding baseline for data protection, electronic transactions and cybercrime
- Ratified by only a minority of member states
- Drafted before cloud computing and AI reached their current scale

**AU Continental AI Strategy (2024)**

- The continental policy frame: Africa-centric, development-focused AI
- Data governance and African-controlled datasets are among its pillars
- A strategy, not a binding instrument
- It depends on national implementation

**Kigali / Africa Declaration on AI (2025)**

- Endorsed by 52 states
- Commitments to African-controlled open datasets and models in critical sectors
- A US$60bn Africa AI Fund
- An Africa AI Council
- Political commitments awaiting institutional machinery

### Pathways for Africa

- Remain as we are — full dependence
- Full-stack national sovereignty
- Fine-tune open-weight models
- Pooled continental compute
- Sovereign data, rented compute

### What Must We Do

- A model AI Law
- Data protection & localization: Be clear on sensitive data classes, and what can be done about them. They also have to be localised.
- Oversight of the Fund
- Talent pipeline: Continue empowering our workforce.
- Energy and water disclosure
- One negotiating position

### Implications for Continental Policy

- AI is factor policy. Its governance belongs with legislatures, not ICT ministries alone.
- Sovereignty is divisible.
- Externalities depend on siting.
- Coordination beats 54 parallel efforts.
- Build institutions, not arsenals.
